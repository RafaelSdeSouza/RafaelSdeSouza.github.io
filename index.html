Inference beyond observed support

A calibration sample may omit regions occupied by the target population. Reweighting changes the mixture only within observed support; the current work asks which population quantities remain bounded when the missing conditional law is restricted explicitly.

What the representation leaves behind

A representation trained for reconstruction or prediction may discard variation needed by a later physical parameter. Current experiments separate losses introduced by the representation from errors introduced by the downstream estimator.

Spectra as ordered geometric objects

spectropath represents a spectral line as an ordered path in velocity–flux space. Profiles with similar widths or moments can remain distinguishable when absorption, asymmetry or multiple components occur in a different sequence along the spectral axis.

Radial structure beyond a single centre

Irregular connected sources may have several plausible centres. RadialPaths measures distance through the observed footprint while retaining the centre assigned to each position, rather than assuming that distance from one centre is sufficient.

Probability models for astronomical observables

Binomial, gamma and negative-binomial models match the probability law to the observable: discrete Population III multiplicity, positive photometric redshifts and globular-cluster counts with more variation than a Poisson model permits.

A high-pitch-angle structure in the Milky Way

Twenty-five star-forming regions trace a narrow structure about one kiloparsec long, inclined by approximately 56 degrees to the Galactic azimuth.

Kuhn et al. (2021)

Coherent regions in resolved galaxies

CAPIVARA groups spaxels by spectral similarity and maps them back onto the galaxy. In five MaNGA systems, the recovered regions were coherent in continuum and emission-line properties without imposing a bulge–disc decomposition.
