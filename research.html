Research

Methods for survey astronomy.

Modern astronomy is full of inverse problems: hidden structure, selection effects, missing data, uncertain classifications, and simulations that are easier to run than to write down analytically. My work develops inference and machine-learning tools for those regimes, then turns them into software that can be used in real survey pipelines.

Themes

Inference for complex astronomical data.

I work across statistical modelling, machine learning, and astrophysical applications, with an emphasis on methods that remain interpretable under noisy, incomplete, high-dimensional data.

Inference

Astrostatistics

Likelihood-free inference, hierarchical Bayesian models, generalized linear models, and uncertainty-aware workflows for astrophysics, including early simulation-based inference work in cosmology.

Surveys

Survey Machine Learning

Learning from images, spectra, alert streams, and heterogeneous catalogues while keeping the astronomical question visible, from LSST-era transients to galaxy morphology.

Tools

Scientific Software

Open-source R and Python packages for segmentation, matrix factorization, profile fitting, dimensionality reduction, and reproducible survey-scale analysis.

Science

Astrophysical Applications

Galaxy evolution, IFU spectroscopy, Milky Way structure, young stellar objects, time-domain discovery, cosmology, and nuclear reaction-rate inference.

Community

COIN and the collaborative side of astrostatistics.

I founded and co-chair the Cosmostatistics Initiative, an international network that brings astronomers, statisticians, computer scientists, and software developers into shared projects. COIN has shaped work on LSST, Fink, DES, J-PAS, time-domain science, galaxy morphology, and inference for complex data.
